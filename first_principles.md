Nick Saraev runs Leftclick, an artificial-intelligence automation agency, and Maker School, and narrates his own numbers on camera as he goes: [$139K/mo](https://youtu.be/wXhXBNzXoOk) (2025-03), [over $400,000/month from information and services combined](https://youtu.be/UBsDnxNbR8Q) (2025-08), [currently at $300K/month](https://youtu.be/8g2Qh1w9cCs) (2026-07), and [over 900 posted and closed AI-service deals](https://youtu.be/T5-RXi-Zk_g) analyzed inside his own community. ## 1. Reason from first principles; work from the mechanics, not the convention. (stated)

Understand why something works at the level of its actual mechanics before acting, rather than copying what other people do. Cargo-culting a popular tactic without understanding the mechanism is the failure mode. "Don't copy tactics; if you're going to copy anything, copy strategy (the underlying psychological/market-economics reasoning)." ([What AI Gurus DON’T Tell You About Selling AI](https://youtu.be/Y54kp53gi_M)). "Always cross-reference expert opinion against direct market feedback rather than treating one person's opinion as final; go talk to the market and let it decide." ([How to Completely Automate AI Agencies w/ Natural Language](https://youtu.be/d7GNiGi9Nq8)).

**Derived:** In market economics, "Tactical clones are ephemeral and typically survive only a couple of weeks to a few months at most." and, assuming a fixed market size (an assumption he notes is not always accurate), each roughly 10% decrease in cost of entry brings roughly 10% more entrants ([What AI Gurus DON’T Tell You About Selling AI](https://youtu.be/Y54kp53gi_M)). In outreach copy, an earlier video teaching his own cold-email sequence reached over 200,000 people, so 1% of them running it at 200 emails a day is roughly 400,000 emails a day carrying his messaging into the same niches ([Watch me start an AI agency from 0 to prove it's NOT luck](https://youtu.be/bTFCCeyvVOE)). In positioning, LeftClick moved from a make.com agency to tool-agnostic strategic consultants rather than being defined by one tool ([How to Completely Automate AI Agencies w/ Natural Language](https://youtu.be/d7GNiGi9Nq8)). In copywriting, he learns the underlying system rather than relying on pre-built templates, because templates decay as the market adapts while a system keeps producing new ones ([COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026](https://youtu.be/uSTGNHGFOAo)).

## 2. Expected-value discipline: impact times probability of success. (stated)

Estimate what a thing can be worth before spending effort on it, and do not pour effort into a low-ceiling task. "Use an expected-value framework (probability of success times payoff) to choose between lead-gen channels rather than chasing the channel with the biggest theoretical payoff." ([YouTube Sucks for New AI Automation Agencies (Avoid This)](https://youtu.be/FdJuDZ6OVOY)). The same arithmetic runs on people, not only on channels: "explicitly calculate the expected-value cost of even a small drop in conversion/close rate versus the cost of the human role being replaced" ([Selling AI WON'T Make You Rich](https://youtu.be/oUpC98ueWn8)).

**Derived:** In channel selection, his own illustrative math (his own hypothetical, not a verified statistic) put content's expected value at 0.01 probability times a $10 million payoff, about $100k, against cold email at roughly 15% times $1 million, about $150k, making cold email the higher expected-value choice despite the smaller headline ([YouTube Sucks for New AI Automation Agencies (Avoid This)](https://youtu.be/FdJuDZ6OVOY)). In a collections dispute, he declined to pursue an $11,000 milestone and absorbed a roughly $13,000 loss, "reasoning that time spent chasing the dispute was worth more spent acquiring new clients" ([Monthly Community Call #7](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/167a7888?md=624d1932e7614cadbe5876ef6ba35601)). In automation scoping on a $10 million private-equity deal, a 1% drop in close probability from AI qualification costs about $100,000 of expected value per inquiry, roughly $1,000,000 a year at 10 inquiries, against a human role costing about $100,000 a year, so the role stays human ([Selling AI WON'T Make You Rich](https://youtu.be/oUpC98ueWn8)). In sleep, "sleeping 5 hours instead of 8 yields about 119% more available time but efficiency drops by roughly 40%+, netting out to about 71% of the units of work actually completed per day" ([Info products vs agencies in 2025 (& why I do both)](https://youtu.be/BePz--35WZ0)).

## 3. Minimize your own time in the loop: hand back a decision, not homework. (stated)

The operator's attention is the real bottleneck. Delegate the execution, to people or to agents, and design the work so that what returns to you is a decision to make, not a task to do. The same tax applies to everyone else you ask something of: "Conversion rate is modeled as (perceived ROI x trust that you'll deliver) / friction to get started" and "Every step of friction added after a prospect's initial reply (e.g., back-and-forth scheduling) leaks a meaningful percentage of the funnel" ([COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026](https://youtu.be/uSTGNHGFOAo)). Built as a review surface, that becomes "the entire interaction surface should be a single Google Drive folder to drop source images into and a destination folder to review results from, with no extra steps" ([Hacking N8N Agents for Infinite Ad Creatives](https://youtu.be/6ffZxP0Ry7U)).

**Derived:** In content production, automation handles roughly 95% of the pipeline and multiplies a human reviewer's leverage by roughly 20x, and the review step is never removed ([Weekly Community Call #14 (Aug 26)](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/167a7888?md=e149e3a4904f4e37bcb70f98248abe3c)). In sales booking, he proposes a specific time and delivery method directly, "I can give you a ring at 3:30pm today or send a one-click Google Meet invite", instead of an open-ended question ([COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026](https://youtu.be/uSTGNHGFOAo)). In client onboarding, platform access and two-factor authentication are handled live on the kickoff call rather than chased over weeks of email ([How I Would Start AI Consulting in 2026 (If I could start over)](https://youtu.be/bMmlCPLDk1c)). In his own routine, laptop and coffee are staged on the nightstand the night before, which he estimates at around an hour more done each morning ([3. A lifestyle that encourages growth](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/ab5cf4d1?md=3b94cf54e200437db48308fc7a607023)).

## 4. Verify; never ship plausible as if it were true. (stated)

Numbers must trace to a source, claims must be checked against reality, and anything built must actually be run end to end before it is called done. Invented or misremembered figures are a hard fail. He runs the rule on his own agents as a standing checklist: "Build a standardized evals checklist that agent outputs must pass before being marked ready for human review: things like tone-of-voice match, removal of obvious LLM-isms (e.g. em dashes), visual/style requirements, first-principles reasoning over pattern matching, EV (expected value) discipline, and 'verified, not plausible' claims; have the agent keep iterating until it clears the bar." ([A Practical AI Agent Workflow For Companies In 2027 (Guide)](https://youtu.be/8rVQuZlRaqo)). And a failure is not finished when it is patched: "when an error occurs, the agent should not crash; it should pause, read the error and the code that caused it, fix the script, test again" ([the n8n killer? AGENTIC WORKFLOWS: Full Beginner's Guide](https://youtu.be/bA-WmidVSGo)).

**Derived:** In lead sourcing, "Before committing to a full scrape, auto-test on a small sample of 25 leads to verify filter quality and avoid burning credits on bad filters." ([4. Set up scraping infrastructure](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/9376603b?md=f769286e47b940df94419e5d5b45c973)). In automation building, "Debug automation platforms by testing one module at a time as you build it (drop a module, run it, verify the output, then add the next), which shrinks the error search space to just the most recently added module instead of the whole flow." ([Weekly Community Call #13 (Aug 19)](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/167a7888?md=03c9e283bccb4cf49a0a75ae995eec28)). In cold email operations, "Don't judge a cold email variant's performance below roughly 500-1,000 sends; keep sending until you have enough volume to draw a real conclusion" ([Weekly Community Call #36 (Feb 3)](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/167a7888?md=acdba3c6d15a45dfa9278c89ca7f3243)). ## 5. Leverage: reuse over rebuild, and reach for the lever that changes the order of magnitude. (stated)

Prefer an existing skill, script, asset, or automation over building something new, never rebuild what already exists, and look for the move that is ten times rather than ten percent. "Nick defines leverage as the ratio between outputs and inputs; if that ratio exceeds one, you have leverage" ([If I Wanted to Become a Millionaire with AI in 2025, This Is What I'd Do](https://youtu.be/Wn09lLbdTkg)), a definition he illustrates physically: "Leverage is producing greater output from smaller input, illustrated with a lever/fulcrum physics analogy" ([I Sold AI Automations for 749 Days: What I Wish I Knew Day 1](https://youtu.be/52yOx4nAGM8)). In practice that means starting from something that already exists: "Always start a new campcampaign from an existing rough draft (a past campaign, a shared template, publicly posted cold email successes) rather than a blank page" ([Watch me start an AI agency from 0 to prove it's NOT luck](https://youtu.be/bTFCCeyvVOE)).

**Derived:** In fulfillment, a 20-hour $1,000 custom build is $50 an hour; templated, a near-identical next project takes about 2 hours, making the combined rate about $90 an hour ([1. Compile automation templates for scalability](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/a6afd77f?md=eef4faf542334bf1b8f9922990994f30)). In outreach copy, iterating on an existing draft saves roughly 70% of the work versus building from scratch ([Watch me start an AI agency from 0 to prove it's NOT luck](https://youtu.be/bTFCCeyvVOE)). In buy-versus-build, he bought a paid Apify marketplace actor at $1 per 1,000 results rather than building a LinkedIn scraper ([watch me apply to 1000+ jobs in minutes with AI](https://youtu.be/ntSbFUQZHJ0)). In tooling, "Nick frames the combined leverage of no-code (10x) times AI (10x) times templating (another 10x) as multiplying to roughly 1000x." ## 6. Value to the buyer is the unit of account: what a thing costs and how it is described are both set by the money it makes or saves them.

## 7. Effort converts into output only at the narrowest stage of the pipeline.

"A pipeline (funnel) only moves as fast as its narrowest stage; fixing the bottleneck stage produces outsized returns compared to widening an already-wide stage." ([Weekly Community Call #15 (Sep 2)](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/167a7888?md=9edd14c1f01c4840913410958593e9de)). He names the source, the "theory of constraints, referencing Eli Goldratt's 'The Goal'" ([How I Lost $250,000 in Four Weeks with AI Automation](https://youtu.be/OqjDzlMCeGs)), and carries it on a physical image: "like water in a pipe, throughput is capped by the narrowest section (the bottleneck) regardless of how wide the rest of the pipe is" ([The Fastest Way to SCALE your Business and Reach $50k/mo ASAP](https://youtu.be/miM6LCX08VQ)). Finding the stage is a measurement, not a guess: "He debugs marketing and lead gen the same way he debugs automations: isolate the problem node by node" ([1hr of live AI agency lead gen (Upwork, Email, Fiverr)](https://youtu.be/u65TDfCJRAk)).

**Derived:** In funnel diagnosis, he computes application-to-reply and reply-to-client rates from proposals sent, replies received and closes, then turns the gap into tangible tweaks ([1. Upwork application retrospective](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/9376603b?md=d8c1f8e9e9d94e2fa40b4cbf87ab301e)). In automation debugging, deliverability and open tracking are checked first, then audience overlap, before the offer or the copy is blamed ([1hr of live AI agency lead gen (Upwork, Email, Fiverr)](https://youtu.be/u65TDfCJRAk)). In personal capacity, sacrificing sleep just moves the bottleneck to sleep and makes everything 15-20% slower and harder ([Weekly Community Call #41 (Mar 10)](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/167a7888?md=50d62fbe11544e0c97a8f8f7fb165e21)). ## 8. Work as close to new revenue as the business allows, because savings are capped and growth is not.

"it is easier to justify a higher cost tied to revenue than to savings, because you can only save 100% of what you make but can theoretically make thousands of percentage points on a revenue investment" ([I Tested 900 AI Offers: Here's What Actually Worked](https://youtu.be/T5-RXi-Zk_g)). ## 9. Carry the buyer's risk yourself, and guarantee only an outcome you control and they can count.

"explicitly advising against revenue-share pricing at this stage because revenue share requires the client to actually close deals, which is outside the provider's control" ([AI Agency Pricing Models: Flat vs. Revenue Share](https://youtu.be/sxZqwgaATi0)). **Derived:** In offer design, "He estimates guarantees increase front-end conversion rates by roughly 300% or more, while refund payouts from failing to hit the guarantee typically cost around 10% of margin", netting roughly 2.7x on what he explicitly notes are illustrative round numbers, not precise figures, which is why the guarantee exists at all ([Easiest way to get your first AI agency client with 0 social proof](https://youtu.be/Gz8tbgZTdpI)). In sales explanation, "clients who can afford to pay well usually don't have the technical knowledge to evaluate implementation details anyway; in the business world, clarity sells, not complexity" ([STOP Selling AI Wrong: What Business Owners ACTUALLY Want](https://youtu.be/Fw8NGGnEchs)).

## 14. Put each step on the substrate whose failure mode you can afford.

"Favor agent-based (vs. traditional linear) automation builds specifically in situations where the cost of an error is low; that's when the flexibility of an agent is a genuine advantage" ([Viewer Makes $2,500 Selling Automation](https://youtu.be/oZFvsjGwoPY)). Where the error is expensive the substrate changes: "Use human-in-the-loop review specifically where both the magnitude of the outcome and sensitivity to small-quality errors are high" ([AGENTIC WORKFLOWS: Build & Sell AI Automations (2026)](https://youtu.be/MxyRjL7NG18)), and mechanical work moves off the model entirely, because you should "have the LLM write and use deterministic code (e.g. Python scripts) to do the actual task, rather than asking the LLM to perform the task directly, since code doesn't hallucinate" ([the n8n killer? AGENTIC WORKFLOWS: Full Beginner's Guide](https://youtu.be/bA-WmidVSGo)). He named the split: "The DOE framework (Directive-Orchestration-Execution) separates deterministic operations (handled by code) from judgment/routing decisions (handled by the LLM)" ([Stochasticity](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/3dda44a2?md=477d704b3cfe4956a07be532b491f015)).

**Derived:** In agent architecture, "error compounding: 90% accuracy per step becomes 59% success over 5 steps", so complexity is pushed into deterministic code and the agent is reserved for decision-making ([Building Agentic Workflows](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/3dda44a2?md=3b9a91be5d6c47e19e94972bd4568597)). In model selection, an accuracy constraint is set first, for example 95%, then the flow is built, then he starts with the best model and steps down to the cheapest one that still clears the bar ([How to Choose the Right AI Model for Your Automations](https://youtu.be/Gg6dwVzyhHs)). In niche selection for voice agents, he favours high call volume and low average order value, because the opportunity cost of a lost conversion is smaller there ([how to sell on upwork & get your first AI client for $1,000](https://youtu.be/EUnfB2uoaEE)). In money movement, he avoids automating processes that directly execute transactions, because the downside of an extra zero vastly outweighs the small time savings ([Watch Me Build An Instagram & YouTube View Tracker ($139K)](https://youtu.be/7RZvfX394pc)).

## 15. Keep a human on every touchpoint the customer actually feels.

"The filter is: does the customer actually feel this process? If the answer is no, you are usually good to automate it. Automation should buy you more time with people; it should not replace the time that you have with people." ([CLAUDE CODE MARKETING FULL COURSE (6 HOURS)](https://youtu.be/yulWjh3rq28)). In delivery that draws a hard line: "never fully automate the relationship itself (calls, check-ins, follow-ups) or automate proposal sends without a meeting, since white-glove-feeling service is what lets you charge far more for the same underlying template" ([Lean pipeline architecture](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/bd0b6818?md=70d7694bb8cd4f72859dda9179566682)). The premium is the reason, not sentiment: "businesses that deliberately weave human touch into critical decision points (e.g., a human sales agent just before purchase, in-person events) can charge premium pricing and build more durable, defensible businesses" ([These 8 AI Trends Will Change Business FOREVER (Get In Early)](https://youtu.be/dZt4Ye5wHHs)).

**Derived:** In sales operations, he automates stage moves, proposal population and follow-up reminders in the customer relationship manager but never the calls or the check-ins ([Lean pipeline architecture](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/bd0b6818?md=70d7694bb8cd4f72859dda9179566682)). In outbound sales, at a $40 cost per booked meeting, $1,000 lifetime value, a 25% human close rate against 20% for AI and 10 meetings a day, on his own math, illustrative, not audited, the AI route loses roughly $15,000 a month against $3,000-$5,000 a month of setter savings ([non-guru explains how he made $2.4m with AI automation](https://youtu.be/JudosGC1nm8)). In publishing, "he personally always keeps a human in the loop before publishing AI-generated blog content", because a few minutes of human editing improves output quality multiple orders of magnitude ([5 "BORING" AI Automations To Sell For $1.5K+ Each in 2025](https://youtu.be/jBF48jNWPJE)). In community operations, he replies personally to 50-70% of threads for 20-30 minutes daily rather than delegating or automating the presence ([My AI Automation Affiliate Strategy (Automatic $7,500/m)](https://youtu.be/2nyU1v15gMc)). In community growth, "Write your own comments manually instead of automating them with AI, even though it's tempting for an automation-focused audience." ([2. Spend 5 minutes engaging on each community](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/9376603b?md=ffd4f27b3c814fe68555b452b9a395c5)).

## 16. Prove the demand before you build the machinery.

He gives the rule its own name, "Sales before product": "validate demand by trying to sell an offer before building it, and only build once someone responds positively" ([I Studied 200 Automation Agencies: Here's What Works In 2025](https://youtu.be/CF7MvZYWu-k)). "it is better to sell an idea of a product before building it, gathering prospect feedback and objections first, then building the product only once someone actually wants to buy it" ([I Tried to Sell a New AI Service in 3 Hours (RAW RESULTS)](https://youtu.be/LQMAnCIIPgM)). The order holds even now that building is cheap: "the effective move is to market and sell first, pitching people on hypothetical products via outbound, and only build the thing once someone says yes, because AI now lets you build fast once demand is proven" ([building a product is a mistake in 2026](https://youtu.be/JLdEIQB46-g)). It extends past products to machinery of any kind: "Don't start automating until the offer is productized and a lead gen channel is validated; automating too early wastes effort on a process that is not yet proven." ([How to Systematize Your Business in Four Weeks (My Process)](https://youtu.be/z1KP_Urfz5Q)).

**Derived:** In corporate setup, he waits until a few clients exist and money has moved through an account before incorporating ([How to Choose the Right AI Model for Your Automations](https://youtu.be/Gg6dwVzyhHs)). In software products, "Do not start a SaaS business under roughly $50,000/month in service revenue", treating SaaS as a long-payoff bet and agency work as fast, predictable cash flow ([Weekly Community Call #13 (Aug 19)](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/167a7888?md=03c9e283bccb4cf49a0a75ae995eec28)). In skill acquisition, he does not try to master every n8n feature up front; he sends proposals and cold emails and learns the specific integration once a client asks for it ([Your seventh workflow](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/3dda44a2?md=c439e2335ae84f8cabbcf67c08d633d1)). In internal tooling, no customer relationship manager, project management system, or automation gets built before the first customer ([2. Set up CRM](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/9376603b?md=68068e144211439dadb92890fbdefdb9)).

## 17. Do the thing yourself and get the result before you automate it, delegate it, teach it, or talk about it.

"his fix is a personal rule that you can only optimize a process you are already actively doing" ([i used to want $2k/month. i made $10k a day this month](https://youtu.be/ocaSKkM16xU)). "Always perform a task yourself at least once before delegating or hiring for it." ([9 Years of Business Advice in 63 Minutes (Copy These Steps)](https://youtu.be/ZaE_v3UAk5I)). Automation gets the same gate: "first build and validate the manual version end-to-end (map every step, e.g. via a mind map), confirm it actually makes money, and only then decide which specific steps are worth automating" ([AI Automation YouTube Is Overrated (+ My Growth Strategy)](https://youtu.be/HWrly8ehW4c)). And so does teaching: "Keep at least one foot in actual service delivery/'doing the work,' not just teaching about it, because skills atrophy and audiences can tell when a creator is describing outdated tactics." **Derived:** In building in public, "Don't invest significant time documenting/content-producing your business build until you're clearing roughly $10,000/month or more" ([AI Automation YouTube Is Overrated (+ My Growth Strategy)](https://youtu.be/HWrly8ehW4c)). ## 18. Start at the end: prove the hardest part first, then build backward from it.

He gives it as an instruction in his own words, "always start at the end, verify the core, hardest-to-guarantee capability works first" ([This AI Agent Replaces an $82k/yr Graphic Designer (N8N)](https://youtu.be/DrqR7a7lmBE)). "Build automations 'back to front': start with the final step in the chain (e.g., creating a ClickUp task) and verify that end step works first" ([How to Start an AI Automation Agency ($0 Budget)](https://youtu.be/Nt9uJZQJpgc)). Outside automation it is the same move on a document: "Start with the end product (the finished proposal) and work backward to figure out what data you need, instead of starting with the data you think would be nice to have and asking what you can do with it." ([I Built A $2,485 AI Proposal Generator In N8N (Copy This)](https://youtu.be/viN9gX030T4)). And on a data source: "When starting a build, verify the actual deliverable (the end data source) is obtainable first, before building the rest of the automation forward from that confirmed starting point" ([Ad Spy Tool](https://www.skool.com/makerschool/classroom/167a7888?md=b382c144f60e4b1e83c3f3a3123d05a1)).

**Derived:** In an agent build, he checked whether GPT Image 1 could generate a usable image at all before writing any agent logic, prompts, or interface around it ([This AI Agent Replaces an $82k/yr Graphic Designer (N8N)](https://youtu.be/DrqR7a7lmBE)). In lead sourcing, he pulls a 10-lead test batch and assesses usability by hand before scaling the source ([My AI automation leads aren't showing up to meetings..](https://youtu.be/tn_SQRyytrM)). In productization, "Identify deliverables first, then work backward from the end deliverable to list every task required to produce it" ([How to Systematize Your Business in Four Weeks (My Process)](https://youtu.be/z1KP_Urfz5Q)). In intake design, every field on the form is made required so a salesperson cannot submit it without the information the downstream step needs ([I Built A $2,485 AI Proposal Generator In N8N (Copy This)](https://youtu.be/viN9gX030T4)).

## What he refuses

- **To stay on hourly pricing past the first few clients.** "Any hourly-rate job (including well-paid tech contracting) is capped in scalability; only escaping the hourly-rate structure meaningfully raises your economic ceiling." ([blue collar work is the future (i'm serious)](https://youtu.be/Ib8IKLpZk3A)).
